Chapter Three: Shi Fu

At four in the afternoon, all three thousand four hundred and twelve accounts were restored to the whitelist. Shen Che's report ran two lines: Cause, model anomaly; Action, restored.

He did not delete the spreadsheet.

At six, Lu Nan came to find him, holding a sticky note. "Shen Ge, I didn't pull any logs," she said first, by way of declaration. "I just went and asked the people in the tech department whether, when Ye Hang trains overnight, someone could slip rules into it."

"What did they say?"

"They can. There's an interface called 'Ren Gong Xian Yan.' In theory, only the head of risk control has access." Lu Nan paused. "Zhou Zong."

Shen Che folded the sticky note and put it in his pocket. "Xiao Lu, this stops with you."

"But—"

"It stops with you." He repeated it, his tone heavier than he intended. Lu Nan's eyes reddened. She said nothing and turned and left.

Shen Che sat in the office until nine, then called a car.

"Driver, to Pudong Airport."

"The airport at this hour?" The driver looked at him in the rearview mirror. "Dropping someone off or picking someone up?"

"Picking someone up."

Gu Heng's flight landed at eleven. In three years they had met only twice. The last time was last winter, when Gu Heng came back to Shanghai to handle some paperwork, and the two of them stood outside the convenience store at the airport drinking a can of hot coffee. Gu Heng said he was working as a consultant for a small company in Hangzhou, and that life was easy. Shen Che did not ask him why he had left Guanlan, and Gu Heng did not say.

But at noon today, Shen Che had sent him the spreadsheet. Gu Heng replied with one line: I'll be there tonight.

The arrivals hall at the airport was nearly empty. Gu Heng came out pulling a small suitcase, a little thinner than last year. He saw Shen Che and smiled.

"A Che, you've grown some teeth."

"Shi Fu." Shen Che took the suitcase. "What you saw three years ago—was it the same thing?"

Gu Heng did not answer right away. They walked out side by side, past the convenience store, to the taxi stand. There were very few people in line.

"Not three thousand," Gu Heng finally said. "Three hundred. And the client wasn't Qingye Life either. But the interface was the same, the window was the same, and the signature was the same—Ye Hang."

"What did you write in your report?"

"Model anomaly," Gu Heng said. "And then I left."

A taxi pulled up in front of them. Gu Heng did not get in. He turned and looked at Shen Che.

"A Che, the thing I didn't finish back then—it doesn't have to be you who finishes it."

"I know," Shen Che said. "But I'm in the Review Group. Seeing what we're not supposed to see—that's our job."

Gu Heng was taken aback for a moment, then laughed. That was what Shen Che had taught Lu Nan, and three years ago, it was what Gu Heng had taught him.

"Get in," Gu Heng said. "Let's go back and get some sleep first. Tomorrow morning at eight, we go see Zhou Zong."
