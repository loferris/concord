Chapter 3: The Master

At four in the afternoon, all three thousand four hundred and twelve accounts had been restored to the whitelist. Shen Che's report was two lines long: Cause, model anomaly; Action, restored.

He didn't delete the spreadsheet.

At six, Lu Nan came to find him, a sticky note in her hand. "Shen-ge, I didn't pull the logs." She stated this upfront. "I just went to ask the people in the tech department whether someone could have inserted rules into Night Voyage during its early-morning training."

"What did they say?"

"They said yes. There's an interface called 'Human Prior.' In theory, only the risk control lead has access." Lu Nan paused. "President Zhou."

Shen Che folded the sticky note and put it in his pocket. "Xiaolu, this matter stops with you."

"But—"

"It stops with you." He repeated it, his tone heavier than he'd intended. Lu Nan's eyes reddened. She didn't say anything, just turned and left.

Shen Che sat in the office until nine, then called a taxi.

"Driver, to Pudong Airport."

"The airport at this hour?" The driver glanced at him in the rearview mirror. "Dropping someone off or picking someone up?"

"Picking someone up."

Gu Heng's flight landed at eleven. In three years, they'd met only twice. The last time was winter of the previous year, when Gu Heng came back to Shanghai to handle some paperwork, and the two of them stood outside a convenience store at the airport drinking a can of hot coffee. Gu Heng said he was working as a consultant at a small company in Hangzhou, taking it easy. Shen Che didn't ask why he'd left Guanlan, and Gu Heng didn't say.

But at noon that day, Shen Che had sent him the spreadsheet. Gu Heng replied with a single line: I'll be there tonight.

The arrivals hall was nearly empty. Gu Heng came out pulling a small suitcase, thinner than last year. He saw Shen Che and smiled.

"A-Che, you've grown some teeth."

"Shifu." Shen Che took the suitcase. "What you saw three years ago—was it the same thing?"

Gu Heng didn't answer right away. They walked side by side toward the exit, past the convenience store, to the taxi queue. There were few people in line.

"Not three thousand," Gu Heng finally said. "Three hundred. And the client wasn't Qingye Life. But the interface was the same, the window was the same, the signature was the same—Night Voyage."

"What did you write in your report?"

"Model anomaly." Gu Heng said. "And then I left."

A taxi pulled up in front of them. Gu Heng didn't get in. He turned to look at Shen Che.

"A-Che, the thing I didn't finish back then—it doesn't have to be you who finishes it."

"I know." Shen Che said. "But I'm in the review team. Seeing what we're not supposed to see—that's our job."

Gu Heng was taken aback for a moment, then laughed. Those were the words Shen Che had taught Lu Nan, and three years ago, Gu Heng had taught them to him.

"Get in." Gu Heng said. "Let's go back and get some sleep first. Tomorrow morning at eight, we go see President Zhou."
