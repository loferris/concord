Chapter 3: Shifu

At four in the afternoon, all three thousand four hundred and twelve accounts were restored to the whitelist. Shen Che's report was two lines long: Cause, model anomaly; Action, restored.

He did not delete the spreadsheet.

At six, Lu Nan came to find him, a sticky note in her hand. "Brother Shen, I didn't pull any logs," she said first, by way of declaration. "I just went and asked the people in the tech department whether, during Ye Hang's early-morning training, someone could slip rules into it."

"What did they say?"

"They can. There's an interface called 'human prior'—in theory, only the risk control lead has permission." Lu Nan paused. "Director Zhou."

Shen Che folded the sticky note and put it in his pocket. "Xiao Lu, this stops with you."

"But—"

"It stops with you." He repeated it, his tone heavier than he intended. Lu Nan's eyes reddened. She said nothing and turned to leave.

Shen Che sat in the office until nine, then called a taxi.

"Driver, to Pudong Airport."

"The airport at this hour?" The driver looked at him in the rearview mirror. "Dropping someone off or picking someone up?"

"Picking someone up."

Gu Heng's flight landed at eleven. In three years they had met only twice. The last time was winter of the previous year, when Gu Heng came back to Shanghai to handle some paperwork, and the two of them stood outside the convenience store at the airport drinking a can of hot coffee. Gu Heng said he was working as a consultant for a small company in Hangzhou, that life was easy. Shen Che did not ask why he had left Guanlan, and Gu Heng did not say.

But at noon today, Shen Che had sent him the spreadsheet. Gu Heng replied with one line: I'll be there tonight.

The arrivals hall at the airport was nearly empty. Gu Heng came out pulling a small suitcase, thinner than last year. He saw Shen Che and smiled.

"Ah Che, you've grown some teeth."

"Shifu." Shen Che took the suitcase. "What you saw three years ago—was it the same thing?"

Gu Heng did not answer right away. They walked side by side toward the exit, past the convenience store, to the taxi stand. There were very few people in line.

"Not three thousand," Gu Heng finally said. "Three hundred. And the client wasn't Qingye Life. But the interface was the same, the window was the same, and the signature was the same—Ye Hang."

"What did you write in your report?"

"Model anomaly," Gu Heng said. "And then I left."

A taxi pulled up in front of them. Gu Heng did not get in. He turned to look at Shen Che.

"Ah Che, the thing I didn't finish back then—it doesn't have to be you who finishes it."

"I know," Shen Che said. "But I'm on the Review Team. Seeing what we're not supposed to see—that's our job."

Gu Heng was taken aback for a moment, then laughed. Those were the words Shen Che had taught Lu Nan, and three years ago, Gu Heng had taught them to him.

"Get in," Gu Heng said. "Let's go back and get some sleep first. Tomorrow morning at eight, we go see Director Zhou."
