Chapter One: Graylist

By the time Shen Che refreshed the backend for the third time, the record was still there.

At 1:17 a.m., only the three people from the Review Team were left in the Guanlan Data office. Outside the window were the lights of Lujiazui; inside, the glow of monitors. Neither kind of light was warm.

"Brother Shen, look at this." Lu Nan slid her chair over. On the screen was a user profile. "This account was on the whitelist yesterday. Today it moved itself to the graylist. Nobody touched it."

That was exactly what the Review Team handled: the company's recommendation system, Ye Hang, tagged two hundred million users every day, and when it tagged one wrong, someone had to change it back. The whitelist was normal users, the blacklist was bots, and the graylist was "can't say for sure." By the rules, accounts on the graylist had to be manually reviewed within seventy-two hours.

"The system changed it itself?" Shen Che asked.

"That's what the log says." Lu Nan opened a line of record. "Operator: Ye Hang."

Shen Che stared at those two words for a long time. Ye Hang was a model, not a person. A model could change tags, but a model wouldn't sign its own name.

"Xiao Lu, pull all the records from the past week where the operator is 'Ye Hang.'"

Lu Nan paused. Six months into the company, Shen Che rarely called her Xiao Lu. Usually he used her full name, or didn't call her anything at all and just pushed a document in front of her.

"How many do you want pulled?"

"All of them."

Twenty minutes later, the spreadsheet was out: three thousand four hundred and twelve records, all changed from the whitelist to the graylist, all between one and two in the morning.

Shen Che sent the spreadsheet to Director Zhou, then added a line after the message: Can we talk for ten minutes tomorrow morning?

Director Zhou's reply popped up almost instantly, as if she hadn't slept either: "Eight o'clock, small meeting room."

Shen Che turned off the screen, and half the office went dark at once. It suddenly occurred to him that those three thousand–plus accounts were all real people. Someone would wake up this morning and find their Ye Hang home page strange, filled with recommendations they couldn't understand, with no idea why.

"Brother Shen," Lu Nan said quietly in the dark, "did we see something we shouldn't have?"

"We're the Review Team," Shen Che said. "Seeing things we shouldn't is our job."
